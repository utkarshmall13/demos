Model. Plain linear soft-margin SVM: minimise ½‖w‖² + C Σi ξi subject to yi(wTxi + b) ≥ 1 − ξi, solved in the dual by sequential minimal optimisation. No kernel or feature expansion is used; support vectors are ringed in black in the left panel. Why C trades bias for variance even for a linear model. For very small C every training point becomes a bounded support vector (αi = C), so w = C Σ yixi is just a multiple of the mean difference μ̂₁ − μ̂₀: the nearest-centroid rule, very stable but biased whenever the Bayes direction Σ−1(μ₁ − μ₀) points elsewhere. For very large C the margin term is negligible and the boundary is dictated by the handful of points that violate it, so a few flipped labels or outliers swing it from sample to sample (high variance). In between the margin acts as a regulariser. When the shared covariance is elongated and not aligned with μ₁ − μ₀ (the ESL §4.3 / Friedman 1989 setting) the bias at small C is large; for spherical classes (Duda & Hart) the two directions coincide and small C is almost free. Datasets. Anisotropic shared-covariance Gaussians; isotropic Gaussians; Tukey's contaminated normal (10% of points drawn with 3× the spread, a classic robustness test); and Gaussian class-conditionals fitted to Fisher's Iris (versicolor vs virginica on petal length and width, pooled covariance, published class means, standard deviations and correlations). All have a linear Bayes boundary, drawn in black. Class prior. π₁ is the probability of class 1; the Bayes boundary shifts by log(π₁/π₀). With exactly balanced classes the fully regularised model predicts the majority class of its own sample, a coin flip, so variance rises again at the far end of the chart; π₁ = 0.65 makes that collapse deterministic, leaving pure bias. A linear model in 2D has only three parameters, so its variance is inherently small: expect the under-regularised end to sit only modestly above the minimum, and the over-regularised end far above it. How this is computed. M independent training sets of N points are drawn (optionally with a fraction of training labels flipped). For each C on a log grid the fitted model's hard prediction fD(x) is evaluated on a uniform 80×80 grid. Writing p(x) for the fraction of training sets predicting class 1 at x and y(x) ∈ {0, 1} for the Bayes-optimal label, Bias²(x) = (p(x) − y(x))², Variance(x) = p(x)(1 − p(x)), and the expected 0-1 error P[fD(x) ≠ y(x)] equals Bias²(x) + Variance(x) exactly, with zero irreducible error. Averaging over the grid gives the curves. Click or drag on the chart to change C.
