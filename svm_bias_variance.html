Model. Soft-margin SVM with RBF kernel K(x, x') = exp(−γ‖x − x'‖²), trained by sequential minimal optimisation. Support vectors are ringed in black in the left panel. The true boundary is always linear, so the kernel gives the model far more capacity than it needs: with large C every misclassified or margin-violating point is expensive, so the boundary wraps itself around individual training points (high variance); with small C the margin term dominates, the boundary becomes very smooth and eventually collapses to a single class everywhere (high bias). Larger γ makes the kernel more local and shifts the whole picture towards higher variance. How this is computed. M independent training sets of N points are drawn (optionally with a fraction of training labels flipped; the overlapping-blobs dataset is noisy by construction). For each C on a log grid the fitted model's hard prediction fD(x) is evaluated on a uniform 80×80 grid. Writing p(x) for the fraction of training sets predicting class 1 at x and y(x) ∈ {0, 1} for the true (Bayes-optimal) label, Bias²(x) = (p(x) − y(x))², Variance(x) = p(x)(1 − p(x)), and the expected 0-1 error P[fD(x) ≠ y(x)] equals Bias²(x) + Variance(x) exactly, with zero irreducible error. Averaging over the grid gives the curves. Click or drag on the chart to change C.
