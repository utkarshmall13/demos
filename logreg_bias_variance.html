Model. Logistic regression on all monomials xayb with a + b ≤ degree, minimising the logistic loss plus (λ/2)‖w‖² (the intercept is not penalised), solved by Newton's method. The true boundary is always linear, so degree ≥ 2 gives the model more capacity than it needs: with tiny λ the extra monomials bend the boundary around the particular training points (high variance), while a large λ shrinks every non-intercept weight towards zero until the model predicts a single class everywhere (high bias). How this is computed. M independent training sets of N points are drawn (optionally with a fraction of training labels flipped; the overlapping-blobs dataset is noisy by construction). For each λ on a log grid the fitted model's hard prediction fD(x) is evaluated on a uniform 80×80 grid. Writing p(x) for the fraction of training sets predicting class 1 at x and y(x) ∈ {0, 1} for the true (Bayes-optimal) label, Bias²(x) = (p(x) − y(x))², Variance(x) = p(x)(1 − p(x)), and the expected 0-1 error P[fD(x) ≠ y(x)] equals Bias²(x) + Variance(x) exactly, with zero irreducible error. Averaging over the grid gives the curves. Click or drag on the chart to change λ. With degree 1 the penalty barely matters: scaling a linear score never changes its sign, so regularisation only shrinks the boundary's tilt and offset.
